## Proof structure for the method section

1. Start from the unit-norm homogeneous problem and substitute the SVD \(A=U\Sigma V^T\).
2. Remove \(U\) from the objective because orthogonal matrices preserve the L2 norm.
3. Change variables with \(y=V^Tx\); the unit-norm constraint is preserved because \(V^T\) is orthogonal.
4. Reduce the problem to minimizing \(\lVert\Sigma y\rVert_2\) over unit vectors \(y\).
5. Use the non-increasing singular values to propose the final coordinate vector as a minimizer.
6. Prove the claim by contradiction: assume a unit vector has a smaller objective, expand the diagonal expression with its \(y_i\) coefficients, and show it cannot be less than the candidate value.
7. Clarify that this is a minimizer, not necessarily the unique minimizer when the smallest singular value has multiplicity.
8. Convert the selected \(y\) back to a solution for \(x\) using the inverse variable substitution.
