One blocker I've had since the start, stopping me from even getting the first post out so far, has been this tendency to overcomplicate things, be too ambitious with a first project, not restrict scope, and be a perfectionist.

For example, when I first started the site, I said something along the lines of "I want to make a full write up of all of ML, explaining it from the foundations up".

This is a stupid idea, for many reasons:
1. Covering "all of ML from the ground up" isn't even a well defined statement, meaning we have no way of proving or disproving that a given set of write ups satisfies that requirement. So, scope creep is naturally going to happen and get out of control
2. You said this as someone who had never even written a single blog post on this website. You've never gone through the logistics, planning, coding, or build phase of even a basic post. You're trying to run before you can work, and you're not Tony Stark
3. You said you wanted to do that write up because you **wanted to have written that post, rather than actually wanting to write that post**. That might sound like the same thing, but I like this idea from [Erik Bernhardsson's post on motivation](https://erikbern.com/2016/05/24/what-is-your-motivation.html).
	1. The gist is this; I want this website, above all else, to contain a quantity of **extremely high quality notes**, rather than just churning out low quality posts all the time. High quality means spending a lot of time thinking about ideas, applying Feynman's method, editing and revising presentation, before even showing a post off. Spending that much time on something that has no material value to me mandates **that I love the process of doing it**.
	2. For me, this naturally happens a lot, but **only when I follow my curiosity and write about things I'm still curious about**. Stuff like ML, which I already understand the foundations of, is stuff that I've **already learned**. Going back over it just since I think it would be great to have such a post isn't a sufficient motivation for me to spend time on it to learn. Instead, when you get curious about something, capitalize on it then and there to write a post. And feel free to write small, specific posts; no one's asking or expecting a damn textbook.

So yeah! Follow curiosity, smart with small tasks, and feel free to target really specific topics rather than being comprehensive.
